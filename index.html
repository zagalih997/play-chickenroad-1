Anatomy of a single run

A round is simple to learn: two buttons do all the work, one to move the chicken and one to bank your result.

  1. Choose a difficulty tier before anything else, since it sets the lane count and the risk on each hop.
  2. Set your stake. Every multiplier in the round applies to that amount, so keep it aligned with your session budget.
  3. Move the chicken forward. Each safe lane locks in a higher multiplier, and the payout meter updates at once.
  4. Choose between one more lane or the collect button. Banking ends the round and pays stake times the current multiplier.
  5. If traffic catches the chicken first, the stake is lost in full, with no partial return.

The math under the feathers

Each lane has a survival chance, and the multiplier for reaching it is roughly the inverse of the combined probability of getting there, reduced by the house margin. That margin is the core of the game: whether you stop after one lane or push on for ten, the long-run value stays a little below your stake.

The practical result is simple: there is no special stopping point that beats the maths. Leaving early swaps top-end value for frequency; staying in swaps frequency for bigger swings. That is a choice about variance, not a way to beat the casino.
